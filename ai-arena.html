The context

A problem worth solving.

A match between AI fighters has no correct output to test against. The useful question is whether the game is balanced, and that cannot be answered by watching one match.

My contribution

What I brought to the work.

Built a deterministic fixed-step simulation, with seeded randomness, a 60 Hz step and physics stepped in a fixed order, that the browser renders and a headless runner batches. Built the fighters' perception, cover, routes, morale and weapon timing.

The product

What it makes possible.

01

Spectator view

A 3D scene with an automatic camera director and a match HUD. Any seed can be replayed, paused or fast-forwarded.

02

Fighter decisions

Perception, cover, routes, morale and weapon timing produce the contest: simulation logic, not chat agents.

03

Balance laboratory

Headless batches report team wins, the attack and defense split, round outcomes and each fighter’s impact; every shot is logged with its probability factors.

04

Decisions from data

470 logged matches across three batches. In the final 100 matches, attackers won 44.9% of rounds, inside the balance gate, while one team still took 63% of matches, an edge recorded rather than hidden.
