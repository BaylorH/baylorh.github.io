The context

A problem worth solving.

A match between AI fighters has no correct output to test against. The useful question is whether the game is balanced, and that cannot be answered by watching one match.

My contribution

What I brought to the work.

Built a deterministic fixed-step simulation, with seeded randomness, a 60 Hz step and physics stepped in a fixed order, that the browser renders and a headless runner batches. Built the fighters' perception, cover, routes, morale and weapon timing.

The product

What it makes possible.

Four parts follow: a match as a spectator sees it, the map, replays, and the balance laboratory.

01

Spectator view

A 3D scene with an automatic camera director and a match HUD. Any seed can be replayed, paused or fast-forwarded.

02

Fighter decisions

Perception, cover, routes, morale and weapon timing produce the contest: simulation logic, not chat agents.

03

Balance laboratory

Headless batches report team wins, the attack and defense split, round outcomes and each fighter’s impact; every shot is logged with its probability factors.

04

Decisions from data

470 logged matches across three batches. In the final 100 matches, attackers won 44.9% of rounds, inside the balance gate, while one team still took 63% of matches, an edge recorded rather than hidden.

The spectator view

Six AI fighters, one automatic camera.

Complete local build · not released

Two teams of three fighters play a bomb-plant mode while a camera director decides what to show: an establishing shot as a round opens, a side-on frame when two fighters duel, slow motion on a kill, an orbit when the match ends. These pictures are the game itself, run on 1 October 2026 from a copy of the final build. The fighters are simulation logic; no language model is involved.

Crossfire District

One map, built for long sight lines and close corners.

The fighters all carry a bolt-action rifle and a knife, so the map decides the contest: open ground to cross, high positions to hold and two bomb sites to fight over.

Seeds

Any second of any match can be reopened.

A match is fully decided by its seed, so the same seed always plays the same match. A moment can be reopened from the page address: which seed, how many seconds in, paused or live, and which fighter the camera follows.

Headless runs

The numbers decide.

The same simulation runs without graphics, a hundred matches at a time. Every shot is logged with the factors behind its hit chance, and a batch reports who wins, how rounds end and what each fighter contributed. These are real outputs of the runner, produced on 1 October 2026.

Where it stands
