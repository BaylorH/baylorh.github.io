The context

A problem worth solving.

A floral consultation produces an event plan: inspiration, colors, flowers and a recipe for every arrangement. The team’s wireframes asked for one place to build it, price it in store packs and give the customer something to keep.

My contribution

What I brought to the work.

Implemented the team’s wireframes as a working web app in March 2026: staff sign-in, the proposal editor, catalog search, the recipe-to-shopping-list calculation, and the customer presentation with print-to-PDF and a pre-filled cart link.

The product

What it makes possible.

Every screen of the app follows in four parts, in the order a consultant uses it.

01

The whole proposal in one place

Customer and event, inspiration photos, a color palette, chosen flowers and a recipe for every arrangement. The editor saves as the consultant works, and any proposal can be duplicated.

02

Flowers from the store catalog

A flower is added by searching the catalog or by asking the embedded AI Floral Assistant, and arrives with its category, pack sizes and prices.

03

Recipes, stem by stem

Each recipe lists its flowers with the stems per piece and the number of pieces to make. Half stems are allowed, and a recipe can be reused from another proposal.

04

A calculated shopping list

Stems per piece times pieces, added across recipes, gives each flower’s need. The app suggests the smallest pack that covers it and shows the surplus or shortfall of the others.

05

What the customer keeps

A presentation of up to 12 pages, from cover and inspiration to recipes and the priced list, printed to PDF from the browser. One button opens a store cart with the chosen packs.

Part one · where a consultant starts

Sign in, and every proposal is on one screen.

A consultant signs in with a company Google account and lands on the dashboard: every saved proposal as a card, and a sidebar that files them by consultant and consultation level. Every picture on this page is the app’s own code running on my machine with invented proposals, invented products and drawn pictures.

Part two · the editor

One long form holds the whole event.

The editor runs top to bottom in the order a consultation does: the customer and event, inspiration and colours, then the flowers. It saves as the consultant works.

Part three · stems to packs

Recipes, stem by stem, become a priced shopping list.

Each arrangement gets a recipe: which flowers, how many stems per piece, how many pieces. The app adds the stems across every recipe and turns them into the store’s packs, with prices.

Part four · what the customer receives

The proposal becomes a presentation and a PDF.

The consultant chooses which pages to include, then opens the presentation: cover, inspiration, flowers, recipes, the shopping list and a day-by-day guide. Download PDF prints it. These pages come from a local run; on the live app the presentation does not open at the moment, and that repair is listed under where it stands.

Engineering choices

The decisions behind the interface.

01

Calculate the list, store the choices.

The shopping list is recalculated from the recipes each time it is shown. Only the consultant’s pack choices are saved with the proposal.

02

Start from the design export.

Five wireframe frames were exported as code on the first day and made interactive the same evening, which gave the team something real to react to.

Built in 74 recorded changes between March 3 and 31, 2026. The shopping-list arithmetic has not yet been checked line by line against real store packs.

Where it stands

Built, with repairs before any launch.

Built in March 2026 and not launched; work paused when the DIY kit migration took priority. Before any launch, the presentation page and product search need repairs, and the shopping list needs a line-by-line check.
