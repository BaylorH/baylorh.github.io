The context

A problem worth solving.

Loan scoring needs to fit into an existing application process, while the team needs visibility into scoring activity and loan performance.

My contribution

What I brought to the work.

Built a machine-learning scoring service that the lender’s application system calls, plus a dashboard for reviewing scoring activity, policy settings, lead sources and loan performance.

The product

What it makes possible.

Five parts follow, one per view of the dashboard. The scoring service itself has no screen; the dashboard shows what it decides. Each screen is a local run of the dashboard’s own code with invented applicants, sources and figures.

Overview · see the activity

Live scoring, and the decision behind each number.

The Overview compares the chosen period with the one before: applications scored, accept rate and average score, then how funded loans went on to perform. Any recent decision opens to what was asked for, the credit scores, the score and the threshold it was held to.

Policy settings · a recorded history

A threshold per state, and a log of every change.

Each state has one minimum score. A change is saved as a recorded action with who made it and when, and the log shows what was observed among loans funded before and after it, with a plain note on what that comparison can and cannot show.

Scenario analysis · explore tradeoffs

Replay history against a stricter threshold.

Moving a state above its current threshold counts what the past would have looked like: loans excluded, profit forgone and losses avoided, and which lead sources the excluded band came from. It is historical evidence, not a forecast.

Lead Sources · compare channels

Where applications come from, and what each source is worth.

Lead Sources follows each source from volume to matured outcomes, and says how much of that volume has an observed result before it ranks anything.

Funded History · follow performance

How funded loans performed over time.

Funded History separates a customer’s first loan from their later ones and follows both by month, state, sequence and timing, with the maturity rule stated on the page.

Engineering choices

The decisions behind the interface.

01

Protect policy changes from conflicting edits.

The policy editor checks the latest saved state before applying a change, preserves unrelated edits and keeps a change history. An adjustment is a recorded action, not an invisible replacement.

02

Separate observation from simulation.

The analysis distinguishes observed funded outcomes from modeled exclusions. A scenario helps explore a decision; it is not presented as proof that a policy change caused an outcome.

These are documented implementation choices. Private policy settings, borrower information and model details are intentionally omitted.

Where it stands

In production, with clear next steps.

Scoring activity updates independently from imported loan-history data. Next steps focus on repeatable data refreshes and more precise links between policy changes and affected loans. The savings figure is my own estimate, not an audited number, and it is one part of the value: the dashboard’s five views show the team how scoring, policy settings and lead sources play out across funded loans.
