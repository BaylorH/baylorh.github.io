The context

A problem worth solving.

Useful estimating context was buried in historical Word proposals, Excel fee workbooks and project-accounting records. AI-assisted extraction makes that information available for research and comparison.

My contribution

What I brought to the work.

Led the engagement through Manifold Engineering, directing the project and managing subcontracted development. Collaborating developers implemented the dashboard and its research tools.

The product

What it makes possible.

Five parts follow: the three sources, how a workbook is read, analytics, the estimating workbench, and a separate briefing prototype.

The research workspace

Proposal history you can search, with the evidence kept.

Delivered with development partners · estimates not yet validated

The workspace brings three kinds of history together: the proposals themselves, the project records from the accounting system, and the Excel fee workbooks. Collaborating developers implemented the dashboard under my direction. These pictures are its own code running on my machine with invented records: no real project, client, person or figure appears, and the name of the client’s accounting system is replaced by a neutral phrase.

Extraction

Spreadsheets become structured records without a model guessing.

Fee workbooks are read by rule: a tool finds the regions of each sheet and types them, and sheets that match a known template become sections. A sheet that matches nothing is kept as raw regions, so nothing is invented to fill a gap.

Across the history

The same records, in aggregate and side by side.

Charts over each source, and a page that sets up to three proposals beside each other. Every figure is the invented dataset’s.

Exploratory

From a new project to a fee, one step at a time.

Exploratory · estimates not yet validated

The workbench walks a new project through six steps: describe it, find similar past work, estimate hours, work out the fee, draft, review. It is exploratory until the source records are reconciled. Two of its steps call a model; no model was called for these pictures, so those steps are shown as their form and waiting state.

A separate prototype · not launched

A morning briefing assembled by a browser assistant.

Prototype · paused before launch

A separate prototype: a personal briefing of priorities, tasks, emails and meetings, filled by a browser assistant that reads the user’s own tools. It was paused before launch. These pictures are its own code with an invented user and invented items.

Where it stands

Delivered, with validation ahead.

The research workspace is delivered. Estimates depend on reconciling the source records first, so the estimating tools are treated as exploratory until that matching is complete. A separate executive-briefing prototype was not launched.
