Broker outreach, being proven one step at a time.

The context

A problem worth solving.

A tenant-representation advisor screens a list of 5 to 30 candidate properties by emailing every listing broker, reading each reply and recording the answers in a tracker. The emails go out under her name, so every message and every recorded figure has to be right.

My contribution

What I brought to the work.

Built the product’s engineering: the advisor’s dashboard and campaign wizard, and the email worker that sends an opening per property, reads broker replies and attachments, writes stated facts to the property’s tracker row and drafts replies in the advisor’s voice. The first beta showed that automated email was not yet meeting that standard, so the beta is paused while each step is proven before advisors rely on it again.

The product

What it is being built to do.

Eight parts follow, in the order an advisor meets the product. Every screen is a local run of today’s build with invented people and addresses: they show what is built. What is proven is counted only on live test campaigns, under Where it stands.

01

Check every row first

The advisor uploads her own spreadsheet. Each property shows the contact and street it will write to before anything is sent, and any row can be left out.

02

One right opening per broker

One email per property, greeted by first name or not at all, carrying the client’s requirements and a plain way to say the space is gone or to offer another building.

03

Facts to the right row

Rent, size, power, doors and clear height go to the property’s own row as the broker stated them, including mixed bases such as yearly rent with monthly charges. Figures for other buildings must never be written there.

04

Attachments read

Flyers, brochures and availability lists are read for the row they belong to, or the advisor is told exactly which file could not be read.

05

Replies in her voice

Questions the outreach already answers are answered without involving the advisor, a missing fact is asked for once, and a finished conversation is closed with thanks.

06

Her decisions, clearly flagged

Call requests, alternative buildings and replies that must wait on an owner become cards that say what happened and what was or was not sent, leaving the choice to her.

Campaign wizard · before anything is sent

Every row is checked before a single email goes.

The advisor uploads her own property list. The wizard shows each row with the contact it will write to, lets her say what to ask for column by column, drafts the opening email in her name, and holds the launch if the message asks for something the columns say not to.

Dashboard · what needs her

One row per campaign, one line per decision.

The dashboard opens on the advisor’s campaigns. A campaign that needs her says so on its own row, and opening it lists each decision by kind and property.

Conversations · facts to the tracker

Each stated fact goes to the tracker; only what is missing is asked for.

A broker answers in pieces. Each figure is written to the property’s own row as he stated it, shown as a chip under his message, and the product asks once, in one sentence, for what is still missing.

Advisor cards · her call

When the decision is hers, the reply is already written.

Tours, other buildings, hand-offs to a colleague, questions only the client can answer: each reaches the advisor as a card that says what happened and what already went out, with a reply she can send as it is or rewrite.

Endings · nothing sent that should not be

A stop is a stop, and the dashboard says so.

A broker who asks to be left alone is stopped and remembered. Stopping a campaign says what it will cancel and what it keeps, and when nothing can be sent the dashboard says why.

Admin · operating the product

Health, spend and limits on one screen each.

The admin pages show whether the scheduled run is healthy, switch campaign creation and automation on and off, track what the product spends by day and kind of work, and let a conversation be read exactly as it happened.

Testing · measured, not assumed

A failing case has a name and a cost.

A harness feeds realistic broker replies through the product’s one reply entry point with the real AI, under a dollar cap. An offline sweep of phrasings costs nothing to run, and every fixed reply is checked against a voice rubric.

Results · after the replies are in

From a filled tracker to a tour packet.

In development · left out of released builds

Once a campaign has its answers, the results workspace turns the selected rows into a summary, a tour packet and a planned route, and tracks each broker’s answer to a tour invitation.

Engineering choices

The decisions behind the interface.

01

Test fixes against the real model.

A harness feeds realistic broker replies through the product’s single reply entry point with the real model, under a dollar cap per run; reading one reply costs about 14 cents. Its first run traced every failure to the rules around the model rather than the model itself.

02

Pin every known fault.

An offline sweep of 871 realistic phrasings costs nothing to run. Each failing case is pinned to a named fault, so a fix shows up as a flipped test instead of passing silently.

03

Prove it live with the fewest emails.

Changes are proven on single-property campaigns between owned test mailboxes, with a fixed email and spend budget. One overnight session ran six such campaigns and shipped four releases for $1.55 of model spend.

More than 10,000 automated backend tests and about 3,800 dashboard and cloud-function tests (local runs, 28 September 2026, with 13 known failures tracked by name). Passing tests are not counted as proof here; only live test campaigns are.

Where it stands

In development, measured honestly.

SiteSift is not open to users today. Each of its eight steps has measurable criteria that count only when proven on a live test campaign against the build that is serving: nine of 22 so far, and no step is complete yet. The beta stays paused until every fault a broker could see is closed.
